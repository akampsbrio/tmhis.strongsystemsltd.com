Technology-Mediated Homeschooling Information System (TMHIS)

A Primary Education (P1–P7) Learning & Governance Platform for the Ugandan Syllabus

🎓 Academic Project Context

This system is designed and implemented as a Plan B Master's Research Project for the Degree of Master of Science in Information Systems (MIS) at Makerere University, Kampala, Uganda.

1. Core Research Problem & Objectives

Homeschooling and home-supported primary education across Uganda face systemic constraints:

  • Curriculum Fragmentation: Need for strict alignment with the National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC) and Ministry of Education and Sports (MoES) competency frameworks for P1 to P7.
  • Parental Pedagogy Gap: Parents facilitating home learning require structured lesson guides, suggested weekly schedules, and teaching tips.
  • Intermittent Connectivity: Rural and peri-urban areas require a robust Offline-First Progressive Web App (PWA) capable of functioning seamlessly during internet blackouts.
  • Verification & Compliance: Curriculum officers and teachers need automated scoring, lesson tracking, and aggregate compliance analytics.

2. The Five System Stakeholders

🎒 Learner (P1–P7)

Accesses curriculum lessons, completes interactive quizzes online/offline, and views personal progress badges.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent / Guardian

Registers learners, downloads daily lesson guides, customizes weekly schedules, and monitors child progress.

👩‍🏫 Supporting Teacher

Oversees assigned home cohorts, grades open-ended assignments, and enters qualitative lesson observations.

🏛️ Curriculum Officer (NCDC)

Sets up curriculum terms, approves learning materials, issues circulars, and tracks national compliance.

Key Backend Design Patterns

  • Front Controller & Dynamic Router: All requests route through index.php and Router.php with parameter extraction.
  • Singleton Database Connection: Enforces strict PDO prepared statements, UTF-8 charset, and Kampala (+03:00) session timezone.
  • Standard Response Formatter: Unified JSON envelopes with proper HTTP status codes.
  • Layered Middleware: Enforces authentication, role barriers, and rate-limiting before requests reach business logic.

PWA Offline Mode & Sync Protocol

Ensuring uninterrupted home education during power and internet outages

1. Service Worker & Application Shell

The Service Worker (sw.js) intercepts all asset requests. It uses a Cache-First with Network Fallback strategy for UI assets (HTML, CSS, JS, fonts) and a Network-First with Offline JSON fallback for API endpoints.

2. IndexedDB Client Storage

While offline, the application stores:

  • Downloaded P1–P7 lesson text, worksheets, and teacher notes.
  • Assessment quizzes and questions.
  • Learner answers and offline attempt progress.
  • Local FIFO Synchronization Queue.

3. Idempotent Synchronization Algorithm

🔒 Idempotency Guarantee

Every mutation performed offline receives a unique client UUID (client_transaction_uuid). If a network timeout occurs during sync and the client retries, the server recognizes the UUID and returns the previous acknowledgment without duplicate processing.

  1. Client detects internet restoration via window.addEventListener('online').
  2. Sync lock is acquired to prevent concurrent sync races.
  3. Client sends oldest pending queue mutation to /api/sync.
  4. Server checks UUID in sync_log table:
    • If processed previously: returns stored ACK immediately.
    • If new: validates payload, verifies authorization, recalculates scores, and commits in an ACID transaction.
  5. Client receives ACK and removes item from local IndexedDB queue.

Module 01: Authentication, Users & RBAC

Status: Completed & Tested

1. Implementation Summary

  • Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Strict 5-role hierarchy (administrator, curriculum_officer, teacher, parent, learner) enforced at both API route middleware and UI rendering layers.
  • Session & Security Governance: Password hashing via PASSWORD_BCRYPT (cost factor 12), time-limited single-use password reset tokens (60 min expiry), and progressive account lockout after 5 consecutive failed login attempts.
  • Administrative User Management: Dedicated administrative control center supporting account creation, status toggles (active, suspended, inactive), role changes, and immediate password reset overrides with audit logging.
  • Immutable Audit Logging: Structured security audit trail capturing user IDs, target IDs, action codes, IP addresses, and before/after metadata snapshots for governance compliance.

Module 03: Curriculum Management (P1–P7)

Status: Completed & Verified (15/15 Tests Passing)

🇺🇬 Uganda National Curriculum Alignment

This module implements the National Curriculum Development Centre (NCDC) and Ministry of Education and Sports (MoES) Primary School Competency-Based Curriculum framework, spanning all 7 primary school grades (Primary One through Primary Seven).

1. Pedagogical Cycle & Curriculum Architecture

The TMHIS curriculum engine models the distinct pedagogical divisions of the Ugandan primary education cycle:

Phase Class Levels Age Guideline Instruction Language Pedagogical Focus & Core Subjects
Lower Primary (Thematic) Primary 1 – Primary 3 (P1–P3) 6 – 9 years Local Language / English Thematic learning (School, Home, Community, Health, Weather), Literacy 1 & 2, Mathematics & Numeracy, Local Language (e.g. Luganda), Religious Education (CRE/IRE), and CAPE.
Transition Year Primary 4 (P4) 9 – 10 years English Language Transition from thematic instruction to structured subject areas: English, Mathematics, Integrated Science, Social Studies (SST), Religious Education, and Kiswahili.
Upper Primary (Subject-Based) Primary 5 – Primary 7 (P5–P7) 10 – 13+ years English Language Advanced subject competencies preparing for national Primary Leaving Examinations (PLE): English, Mathematics, Integrated Science, Social Studies, CRE/IRE, and Kiswahili.

2. Implemented Features & Technical Architecture

  • NCDC Syllabus Seeding (Version NCDC-2026.1): Seeded 224 active, sequenced lessons across 53+ subjects for all 7 primary classes, complete with duration, version identifiers, and competency-based milestone statements.
  • Strict Class-Subject Consistency Guard: Database and API enforcement guarantees that every lesson belongs strictly to the same class level as its parent subject (lesson.class_id === subject.class_id), preventing cross-level anomalies.
  • Learner Curriculum Boundary Isolation: Strict boundary isolation ensuring that learners enrolled in a specific class (e.g., P4) cannot access or leak lessons and quizzes from other classes (e.g., P5), verified by automated security tests.
  • Collision-Free Sequence Reordering: Two-phase batch sequence reordering with offset shifting (`UPDATE ... SET sequence_number = sequence_number + 100000`), guaranteeing zero unique constraint collisions on (subject_id, sequence_number) during lesson moves.
  • Soft-Retirement & Historical Data Preservation: Deprecating outdated or superseded lessons marks them as retired while preserving learner historical quiz scores, progress logs, and audit trails.
  • Officer & Administrator Authoring Suite: Intuitive authoring modals in the PWA enabling curriculum officers to create/update subjects, add/edit sequenced lessons, modify durations, and reorder teaching pathways.
  • Real-Time PWA Curriculum Explorer: User-friendly interface with class-level tabs (P1–P7), subject cards with weekly hours, sequenced lesson timelines, and instant keyword searching across topics and competencies.

1. Architectural Principles

Module 03 implements Uganda's national primary curriculum standard with the following guarantees:

  • Strict Hierarchy: Every curriculum unit is strictly bound: Class (P1–P7) → Subject → Sequenced Lesson.
  • Class Consistency Validation: Subject creation and lesson allocation prevent cross-class contamination.

2. Division Determination & F9 Demotion Rules

  • Division 1 (Aggregate 4 to 12): Requires passing all 4 core subjects (Grade 8 or better) with Zero F9s. Candidate must pass English and Mathematics.
  • Crucial Demotion Rule: If a candidate attains an Aggregate between 4 and 12 but receives 1 or more F9s (e.g. 1, 1, 1, 9 = Aggregate 12), the candidate is STRICTLY DEMOTED to Division 2.
  • Division 2 (Aggregate 13 to 24): Requires at least 3 passes and passing English or Math.
  • Division 3 (Aggregate 25 to 28): Requires at least 3 passes.
  • Division 4 (Aggregate 29 to 32): Requires at least 2 passes.
  • Division U (Aggregate 33 to 36): Ungraded / fewer than 2 passes.
  • Division X: Absent in one or more papers.
